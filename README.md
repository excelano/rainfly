# Rainfly

Rainfly answers one question at camp: should the rain fly go on tonight? It reads the night's forecast for your campsite and gives a yes or no with the reason, such as "Dew likely by 4 AM" or "Rain 35% after 2 AM", and in the morning it estimates when the tent will be dry enough to pack. It runs on iPhone and on Apple Watch, each a complete app without the other, with no account, no ads and no analytics.

It is deliberately narrow. There is no radar, no weather map, no hourly chart and no week ahead, only what tonight means for your gear.

## Using it

The main screen shows the verdict for tonight, or for this morning between sunrise and the time you plan to break camp. "Rainfly on" means at least one check fired, and the line under it names the one that matters most and when. "Rainfly off" means every check is clear. Notes below that suggest opening the vents when the fly is on and the air is damp or still, warn when the night will be colder than your sleeping bag's comfort rating, and give the time the tent should be dry. The camp and the age of the forecast are shown with it, the age turning orange after twelve hours and red after a day. The iPhone lists every check under the verdict, the ones that fired first, each with its symbol, its time and the night's value against its threshold, and tapping one tells why it matters and when it puts the fly on; the night's low, closest dew-point gap and highest rain chance follow. On the watch, All checks under the verdict opens the same list. On first launch the iPhone introduces itself and starts from your location or from a camp you add.

The checks are independent, and any one of them puts the fly on: thunderstorms overnight or before you break camp, rain during the night, rain carried by strong wind, frost or dew on a clear and calm night, rain in the morning before you pack, and a cold night. Each threshold can be changed in Settings under Checks, grouped by the check it belongs to, and Reset to defaults puts them back; Settings also holds the units, the break-camp time and the bag rating.

Forecasts are fetched while the device has a connection and kept on it, so the verdict holds in the backcountry. Camps holds the places you sleep. My Location is the device's own position. A camp can be added by searching for a campground, park, town or address, from the current position, or by coordinates in degrees and decimal minutes, entered with the Digital Crown on the watch or typed on the iPhone, which also takes decimal degrees and can place a camp by panning a map under a pin. Tapping a camp shows it from tonight on and leaves the trip as it is. The trip plans a camp for each night ahead, the main screen switches to it on those evenings, and Download trip fetches every night within a week so they are ready when the signal is gone. On the iPhone, each downloaded night of the trip shows its verdict and opens to its checks.

With both apps installed, the iPhone and the watch keep the same camps, trip, shown camp and settings, and a night downloaded on one is handed to the other, so a trip downloaded on the phone at home is on the watch at the trailhead. Each keeps its own My Location. When both have changed something while apart, each camp, night of the trip and setting keeps whichever change was made later, so changes to different things all survive.

The verdict also sits on the watch face as a complication in the circular, corner, rectangular and inline slots, and on the iPhone as a Home Screen widget and on the Lock Screen; in the morning they switch to the dry-by time. With the pressure alert on, the device reads its barometer on background refreshes through the night and sends a notification when the pressure falls fast while the fly is off. watchOS grants an app a few background refreshes an hour, more when its complication is on the active face, and iOS grants the phone fewer and less regular ones, so this is coarse sampling rather than continuous monitoring. While the watch has its own pressure alert on, the phone samples without warning, so one fall brings one notification.

The dew check is the forecast other weather apps do not make, so the iPhone can keep a dew diary to check it against your own mornings. Turned on in Settings, it keeps each night's forecasts, and from sunrise the main screen asks what you found on something left under the sky: dry, light dew, wet, frost or rain, and whether it stood under open sky. Once the night is over, the phone fetches it again from WeatherKit's history. The diary screen counts the nights the dew check caught, cleared, missed or raised a false alarm on, says for each miss whether the forecast was wrong about the night or the settings called a correctly forecast night wrong, and, once it holds enough open-sky nights of both kinds, suggests the dew-point gap that would have done best, which one tap applies. The diary stays on the phone unless you turn on Share with Excelano, which sends each answered night to Excelano's database in Apple's iCloud to help improve the dew check: what was found, the night's forecast and history, the thresholds, the camp's position rounded to 0.1° and a random diary ID, but no camp names or exact positions. A new diary ID and deleting the shared nights are on the diary screen.

## Building

Open `Zeltwetter.xcodeproj` in Xcode. The `Zeltwetter` scheme builds and archives the iPhone app, which embeds the watch app. The `Zeltwetter Watch App` scheme builds the watch app for a watch or the simulator and runs the tests. Code both apps use lives in `Shared/`, the iPhone's own in `Phone/` and the watch's in `Watch/`. The widgets are extensions of their own, `ZeltwetterPhoneWidgets` in `PhoneWidgets/` and `ZeltwetterWidgets` in `WatchWidgets/`, each sharing the forecast cache and settings with its app through an App Group.

The identity a fork changes, meaning the team id, the bundle ids, the App Group and the display name, lives in `Config/Zeltwetter.xcconfig`, and the version in `Config/Version.xcconfig`. The iPhone and watch App IDs need the WeatherKit capability and service and the App Group, and the iPhone widgets' App ID needs the App Group. The project has no dependencies beyond Apple's frameworks.

The rule engine in `Shared/Engine` uses Foundation alone and is covered by the tests, as are the sync rules in `Shared/Sync`. A Debug build launched with `--demo` and one of `clear`, `rain`, `storm`, `dew` or `frost` replaces WeatherKit and the location with a fixed two-night trip whose first night gives that verdict, and `--open` also opens a screen; `Shared/Support/Demo.swift` has the details. `tools/dewdiary` is a command-line tool, built from the same engine and judging code, that reads the shared dew diary nights from CloudKit with Excelano's server-to-server key and reports how each dew spread would have done on them; `install.sh` there builds it into `~/bin`. Sync between the apps can be checked on a paired iPhone and watch simulator for the shared state, but WatchConnectivity does not deliver handed-on nights in Simulator, so that part needs real devices.

## Privacy

Rainfly sends a camp's position to Apple's WeatherKit for its forecast, and to Apple's map service when a camp is added or searched for. It reads your location while it is on screen, with permission, and the barometer during the night when the pressure alert is on. Forecasts, camps, the trip, settings, recent pressure readings and the dew diary stay on your devices, passing between your iPhone and your watch over WatchConnectivity when both apps are installed. The one exception is a dew diary you choose to share, whose answered nights go to Excelano's CloudKit database as described above. The privacy policy is at https://excelano.com/legal/#rainfly.
