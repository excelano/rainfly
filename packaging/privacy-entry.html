Rainfly (iPhone and Apple Watch) — Privacy

Rainfly tells you, on iPhone and on Apple Watch, whether the rainfly belongs on your tent tonight. To answer, it asks Apple for a forecast at your campsite. This section says what goes to Apple, what the apps keep, what passes between your phone and your watch, what reaches Excelano from a shared dew diary, and how you can check all of it against the source code at github.com/excelano/rainfly.

Forecasts come from Apple

Each forecast is a request from the phone or the watch to Apple's WeatherKit carrying one camp's coordinates: the device's own position for the camp called My Location, or the coordinates of a camp you saved. When you add a camp, its coordinates or the text you searched for go to Apple's map service, to suggest a name, find the camp's time zone, or list matching places, and the iPhone's map for placing a camp is drawn by the same service. The detail screen loads the Apple Weather mark from the address WeatherKit supplies, because Apple's terms require it to be shown. All of these go through Apple's system frameworks under Apple's privacy terms. Excelano runs no server for Rainfly, and nothing about these requests reaches the developer. Rainfly contains no analytics, advertising, crash reporting or third-party code, and it has no account to create. The only data that can reach Excelano is a shared dew diary, described below; with the dew diary or its sharing off, nothing does.

Your position

Rainfly reads your location only while it is on screen and only with your permission, to forecast for where you stand. Without that permission, camps can still be added by search, on the map or by coordinates.

The barometer at night

With your Motion & Fitness permission and the pressure alert turned on in Settings, Rainfly reads the device's barometer for a few seconds at a time during background refreshes between sunset and sunrise. A fast fall in pressure while the verdict is “Rainfly off” raises a notification that the device itself posts; no push service is involved.

What each device keeps

The fetched forecasts, the camps you save with their names and coordinates, the nights you plan at them, your settings, the last twelve hours of barometer readings and the time of the last pressure warning. On each device they sit in storage the app shares with its own widgets or complication, and they go when the app is deleted. A forecast is dropped a day after its night ends, and deleting a camp removes its planned nights with it.

The dew diary

The dew diary is off until you turn it on in the iPhone's Settings. While it is on, it keeps the forecasts fetched for each night and what you answer about the morning after, and for each night that is over it asks WeatherKit once or twice for that night's past hours at the same camp's coordinates. Unless you share it, the diary stays on the iPhone, is never sent to the watch or anywhere else, and goes when you delete its nights or the app. With your permission for notifications and the morning reminder on, the iPhone schedules a notification for itself before break camp, asking what you found; no push service is involved.

Sharing your dew diary

When you turn on the dew diary, Share with Excelano is on with it, as the welcome screens say, and you can turn it off under the dew diary in the iPhone's Settings. While both are on, each night you answer is sent to a database Excelano keeps in Apple's iCloud (the public database of the CloudKit container iCloud.com.excelano.zeltwetter), so the dew forecast can be improved from many campers' mornings. A shared night holds the night's date; the camp's position rounded to a tenth of a degree, about 11 km, and its time zone; what you found and whether it was under open sky; the forecast's hours and the hours WeatherKit later reported for the night; the thresholds you had set; the app's version; and a diary ID, a random code the iPhone makes the first time it shares a night, so that the nights from one place can be told apart from another's. Camp names, exact positions, nights you did not answer, and anything about you or your device are never sent. The diary ID is not tied to your name, your Apple Account or your device, and Excelano cannot learn who you are from it; iCloud itself requires you to be signed in to send a record, and Apple keeps the account behind each record to itself under its own privacy terms. Excelano uses shared nights only to study and improve the dew forecast, never sells or shares them, and never uses them for advertising or tracking. Because each night carries the diary ID, the App Store lists this as data linked to an identifier.

The diary screen shows when a night was last sent. New diary ID replaces the code, so nights sent afterwards cannot be linked to the ones before. Delete shared nights removes from Excelano's database every night your iPhone sent, and deleting a night in the diary removes its shared copy too. Turning sharing off stops sending; what was already sent stays until you delete it. To have shared nights removed that the app can no longer reach, for example after deleting it, write to hello@excelano.com with your diary ID, which the diary screen shows while sharing is on.

Between your phone and your watch

When Rainfly is installed on both your iPhone and its paired Apple Watch, the two keep each other up to date through WatchConnectivity, Apple's framework for an iPhone and its paired watch: your saved camps, the planned nights, the camp being shown, your settings and units, whether the watch's pressure alert is on, and the forecasts fetched for your saved camps. Nothing else is sent. Your position for My Location, its forecasts and the barometer readings stay on the device that took them. These transfers run between your own devices; they do not pass through any Excelano server, and the developer never sees them.

Apple's figures for developers

Apple counts installs on its own, and when Share With App Developers is on, it passes the developer aggregate, anonymous crash logs. Rainfly has no code that touches either. The switch is on the iPhone at Settings > Privacy & Security > Analytics & Improvements and covers every app.

Checking the code

Every network request is made by an Apple framework: WeatherService in WeatherKitProvider.swift, CLGeocoder and MKLocalSearch in CampPlace.swift, the map in AddCampScreen.swift, and the AsyncImage for the Apple Weather mark in AttributionView.swift. The dew diary is kept by DiaryRecorder.swift and fetches past nights through the same WeatherKitProvider.swift; a shared night is built in SharedNight.swift, which holds every field it carries, and sent through Apple's CloudKit in DiaryUploader.swift. What passes between the phone and the watch is sent in SyncSession.swift and described by SyncedState.swift. The code makes no URLSession call of its own. Everything stored is written through Store.swift and SettingsStore.swift. This section is maintained as packaging/privacy-entry.html in the repository, and its git log is the record of every change.
